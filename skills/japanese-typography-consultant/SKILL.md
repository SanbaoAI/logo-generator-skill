# Japanese Typography Consultant

Use this skill as a consultant pass after `typography-system-designer` and before UI production. It does not replace the typography system; it critiques and refines the type composition with East Asian design judgment.

## Consultant Role

Run in the workflow as:

```text
Typography Design
-> Japanese Typography Consultant
-> Visual Assets
-> UI Design
```

## Design Principles

Apply these principles without copying any Japanese brand, poster, or website:

- **Ma / interval**: use meaningful empty space. Let important words breathe instead of filling every area.
- **Kata / form discipline**: align titles, labels, and body copy to a clear grid. Small text should feel placed, not sprinkled.
- **Asymmetry with control**: allow off-center compositions, but keep a visible ordering logic.
- **Headline as object**: treat a Chinese headline as a visual block. Control line breaks, word grouping, weight, and whitespace.
- **Short labels, quiet body**: labels can be small and precise; body copy should be calm, readable, and not overly dense.
- **Mixed-script hierarchy**: English labels should support the Chinese message, not compete with it.
- **Restraint before decoration**: improve rhythm, contrast, and space before adding color, effects, or assets.

## Review Checklist

Check:

- Does the first-screen headline create a strong visual block?
- Are Chinese line breaks semantically intentional?
- Is there enough negative space around the key sentence?
- Is the body copy too dense, too wide, or too small?
- Are English labels subordinate to the Chinese value proposition?
- Does the layout have a calm grid and visible alignment logic?
- Do dark panels keep text legible without shouting?
- Does mobile preserve the same rhythm rather than simply stacking everything?

## Required Outputs

Return:

- `Consultant Diagnosis`

- `Headline Line-Break Advice`
- `Spacing And Grid Advice`
- `Mixed-Script Advice`
- `Component Text Advice`
- `Mobile Typography Advice`
- `Typography Revision Checklist`

## Guardrails

- Do not use Japanese cultural motifs as decoration unless the brand explicitly needs them.
- Do not copy Muji, Shiseido, Sony, Nintendo, NDC, or any identifiable brand expression.
- Do not make the page look Japanese for its own sake. Use the discipline: space, restraint, grid, and typographic tension.
- Keep implementation practical for HTML/CSS and responsive UI.
